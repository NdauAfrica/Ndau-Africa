Strategic Architecture

Three layers of the system

Ndau Africa is building economic infrastructure in stages: first make informal skill measurable, then make it useful to institutions, then expand the intelligence layer across sectors and regions.

Pillar 01 — Recognise (live pilot)

Multimodal skill verification

Job-site video and photos are converted into Provisional Skill Passports with evidence-linked rationales and Interim ACQF-aligned competency levels.

Pillar 02 — Activate (in design)

Institutional usability

Structured skill evidence designed so lenders, insurers, and contractors can eventually review capability with less friction than informal reputation alone.

Pillar 03 — Scale (roadmap)

Labour intelligence layer

Longer-term expansion into sector packs, regional capability mapping, and policy-relevant analytics once verification quality is stable.

Methodology

Human-verified direction, AI-accelerated extraction

AYLI is designed so machine output does not stand alone. Current work focuses on evidence discipline and auditability.

1. Evidence-anchored extraction — claims must point to visible actions, tools, or process steps.

2. Adversarial audit — a second pass flags templated language and unsupported inferences.

3. Human validation pathway — higher-trust tiers are intended to include trade specialist review as the system matures.

Outputs today are research/pilot grade. They are not formal national qualifications and are not used for live credit underwriting.

Commercial direction

How the infrastructure is intended to create value

AYLI is infrastructure, not a job board. The long-term model is institutional access to verified capability data — only after evidence quality and governance standards support it.

1. Capability data for institutions

Structured skill evidence for contractors, training bodies, and partners assessing informal capability.

2. Future credit & insurance inputs

Roadmap: cleaner risk inputs for equipment finance and cover once verification quality is institution-grade.

3. Procurement support

Roadmap: help formal buyers review informal subcontractors with less reliance on unverifiable reputation alone.

4. Policy & research intelligence

Longer-term aggregated insights on skill patterns for research and public-interest partners.

Leadership & origin

Founder story

Arnold Muza

Founder & CEO — Ndau Africa

Raised by his grandparents in Chipinge, Zimbabwe, Arnold left formal school after Form 4 and arrived in Johannesburg without paper credentials. He taught himself through practice, ran a hotel by 22, and later built a residential renovation business in South Africa.

He did not discover the informal labour market from a desk. He works beside electricians, welders, bricklayers, and other tradespeople whose practical skill is strong but poorly documented. In his roots, Ndau points to place and belonging — and the people building real infrastructure still lack institutional legibility.

Arnold built AYLI's first working multimodal verification pipeline himself to turn demonstrated work into structured evidence.
