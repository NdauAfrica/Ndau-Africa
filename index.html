Vision & Infrastructure

What We're Building

Ndau Africa builds AYLI — a multimodal AI protocol that verifies the real, demonstrated skills of Africa's informal workers and informal business owners, mapping evidence from job-site video and photos to the African Continental Qualifications Framework (ACQF).

600 million economically active Africans remain invisible to formal employment, credit, and contracts — not because they lack skill, but because no trusted system exists to verify what they know. AYLI closes that gap by turning real work into auditable, institution-ready data.

We are infrastructure, not a marketplace. AYLI is built to make Africa's informal skilled economy legible to lenders, insurers, and institutions — the trust layer that unlocks tools, credit, and formal opportunity for millions currently locked out.

For Workers

Methodology

Human-Verified, AI-Accelerated

AYLI does not let a single AI pass decide a worker's credential. Every output moves through a layered verification discipline before it is trusted.

1

Evidence-anchored extraction. The model must point to specific visible actions, tools, or moments before assigning any competency level — never a generic assessment.

2

Adversarial audit. A second, independent model pass actively critiques the first — flagging generic language, unsupported claims, or inferred autonomy the evidence doesn't actually show.

3

Human expert validation. Higher-tier credentials require sign-off from a qualified trade practitioner — the AI accelerates evidence review, it does not replace human judgment.

Data governance: Built with POPIA-aligned data practices and explicit worker consent at the point of every capture. Verified data is never used for financial underwriting without institutional-grade safeguards in place.
Leadership

The Founder

Arnold Muza

Founder & CEO — Ndau Africa

Arnold was raised by his grandparents in Chipinge, Zimbabwe, while his mother worked alone in South Africa to support their family. He left formal school after Form 4, arrived in Johannesburg with no professional qualifications, and taught himself hotel management — running a hotel by the age of 22.

Today Arnold runs a home renovations company, working daily alongside electricians, welders, and builders whose skill is extraordinary but who have never had a single document proving what they know. That gap is not abstract to him — it is the people he works beside every day, and it is the reason Ndau Africa exists.

Arnold built AYLI's first working AI verification pipeline himself — direct, ongoing exposure to the informal economy that provides a founder-market fit that cannot be hired for.
